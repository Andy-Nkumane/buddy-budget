# PAIA and Information Officer assessment

Reviewed on 18 October 2026 against current Information Regulator and Department of Justice material. This is not a published PAIA manual or legal advice.

## Preliminary applicability

PAIA defines a private body broadly to include a natural person carrying on a trade, business or profession in that capacity, a partnership, and an existing or former juristic person. The repository does not establish who operates Buddy Budget, their legal form, whether this experimental activity is carried on as a trade/business/profession, or the principal place of business. The exact private-body identity is therefore **OWNER-DEPENDENT** and requires legal review.

If Buddy Budget is operated by a PAIA private body:

- section 50 may permit access to a record required for the exercise or protection of rights, subject to PAIA procedures and refusal grounds;
- section 51 requires a manual containing prescribed body, access, record-category, legislative, POPIA-processing and contact information;
- the manual must be made available through the prescribed channels, including the body's website where it has one;
- prescribed request and outcome processes apply; and
- current Regulator guidance states that the temporary private-body manual exemption ended on 31 December 2021 and private bodies are no longer generally exempt from compiling a manual.

No current Buddy Budget exemption has been evidenced. Do not rely on company size, non-commercial status, tester-only positioning, or an expired exemption without a current Gazette notice and legal review specific to the operator.

## Information Officer position

The Information Regulator states that the head of a private body is Information Officer by virtue of position and that public/private bodies must register their Information Officers. Its guidance states registration is required before the officer takes up POPIA duties. Deputy designation depends on accessibility, size and structure and does not replace the accountable Information Officer.

The repository does not verify:

- responsible party/private-body legal identity or head;
- Information Officer or Deputy identity, authorisation or registration;
- eServices organisation record or certificate/reference;
- principal place of business or service address;
- monitored PAIA/privacy request channel; or
- request register, annual reporting owner, fees, forms, refusal review or complaint workflow.

These are publication and deployment blockers, not placeholders to fill with developer details.

## Manual readiness checklist

A legally reviewed section 51 manual should use the Regulator's current private-body template and verified facts, including:

- body name, legal form, registration details where applicable, addresses and head/Information Officer contacts;
- current Information Regulator PAIA guide availability;
- records available under other legislation and any voluntarily/automatically available categories;
- sufficient description of subjects and categories of records held;
- request procedure, current prescribed Form 2, fees, response/refusal and complaint routes;
- POPIA purposes, data-subject categories, information categories, recipients, cross-border flows and security measures at an appropriate non-sensitive level;
- website and principal-place availability; and
- version, approval, review date and accountable owner.

The [data inventory](data-inventory.md), [data-flow map](data-flow-map.md), [processor register](processor-register.md), and [transfer assessment](international-transfer-assessment.md) are inputs, not a substitute for the approved manual.

## Request and reporting operations

1. Publish only a verified monitored channel and current approved manual.
2. Receive requests through the prescribed process; minimise identity evidence and restrict it to authorised reviewers.
3. Record receipt, verification, scope, searches, fees, decisions, reasons, disclosures, extensions and complaints without placing request content in public issues or ordinary application logs.
4. Apply mandatory/discretionary refusal grounds and third-party procedures only with trained/legal review; do not use privacy or security as a blanket refusal.
5. Maintain the annual-report information requested by the Regulator. Verify the live reporting notice and portal each cycle rather than hard-coding a future deadline.
6. Coordinate PAIA access with POPIA section 23 access/correction routes without treating portability export as complete legal compliance.
